        Looping basics

        Beats, bars and loops

        A beat is one tap of your foot. A bar is a group of beats, usually four: 1-2-3-4. A loop is a few bars that repeat forever. Here every loop is 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars long, so they always line up with each other.

        How long should a loop be?

        • 1 bar is a groove: a beatbox pattern, or a "dum-dum" bass you repeat.
        • 2 bars is a riff: a bassline or rhythm with a little call and answer.
        • 4 bars is the sweet spot: a chord pattern or a short tune. Most songs think in fours.
        • 8 bars is a whole phrase, like a verse melody. Lovely, but harder to nail in one go, so try 4 first.

        A good mix: one longer loop as the backbone, shorter loops for rhythm on top.

        Build it in layers

        1. Bass: a low hum or "bom… bom".
        2. Rhythm: mouth percussion, clicks, "ts" and "k" sounds.
        3. Harmony: long "oohs" and "aahs" that sit underneath.
        4. A hook: a short tune people would hum back.
        5. Sing on top: words, riffs, anything. This part doesn't loop, so it can wander.

        Leave space

        Yes, space is your friend. Every layer adds up, so keep each one simple and leave gaps; a rest in one loop is room for another. Use different heights, low, middle and high: the pitch strip on the right of each pad shows where it sits, so spread them out. If it sounds muddy, take a loop out rather than adding another.

        Timing

        Come in right on the "1", when the ring turns red. The ring turns white for the last bar: that's your cue to finish your phrase and take a breath. Slightly early or late? Hold the pad and use Trim. Headphones stop the loops leaking into the mic.

        Make it a song

        Songs come from loops coming in and dropping out. Try: one loop → build up → everything → drop to one or two → all back in → end on one. 🎼 Song lets you plan that, then play it hands-free while you sing on top.

        What's "eight-track"?

        Before computers, studios recorded onto tape machines with separate tracks. An 8-track could record eight parts separately, then mix them together. Your eight pads work the same way: eight separate parts you can bring in and out. (There was also the 1970s 8-track cartridge, a tape for car stereos.)

        Loopsy Doopsy

        Hum, sing and stack your voice into songs.

        1. Tap a pad and hum or sing. It counts you in, records, then loops it.
        2. The ring turns white in the last bar, so you know when to finish.
        3. Stack a few layers, then tap Sing on top.

        🎧 Headphones help: the loops won't leak into your mic.
